# Poster taste — what gets approved, what gets killed

Read side by side from the founder's decisions of 2026-09-24..10-01: 17
approvals, 5 kills, 2 revisions. The kills and revisions came with no note,
so the reasons below are INFERRED from what the approved posters share and
the killed ones lack. Edit this file to correct them; the Motion Director and
the poster judge load it on every run, and the judge is also shown the
killed posters themselves.

When any rule — here, in `creative-rules.md` or learned by the Coach — says
one thing and the posters the founder approved do another, the approved
posters win. (An approved split-contrast poster has an arrow between its two
cards; a learned rule against arrows does not override that.)

## Shape and surface (founder, 2026-10-02, surface 2026-10-06)

The founder locked this shape: "ye format bahut better hai, pasand aaya
sabko — aisi hi bane, sirf aisi nahi." Every explainer uses the shape. On
2026-10-06 they stopped the surface: glassmorphism does not appear. Cards
are opaque. No frost, no blur, no see-through fill, no glowing glass border.

- Logo, small, top centre.
- A big headline a trader would say, one phrase in the pink-to-violet gradient.
- One grey subtitle under it.
- The diagram the layout describes. A contrast is two large cards and one
  arrow: the left is the problem (red, cracked, draining or flat at zero),
  the right is Vanna (violet, holding, rising).
- Inside it, a mechanism you can read at a glance — a meter, a falling or
  rising chart, a crack through a position, a flow — with short labels.
- One opaque surface, and not the same one as the last poster: solid matte
  cards, a thin-line diagram on the open ground, or hard ink blocks.
  Never frosted glass.
- A footer centred at the bottom.

The locked references are the quality bar for this shape. Copy the shape and
the density, not their mistakes: a label printed twice, a coin or another
company's logo, the word "never", or "testnet" on a Solana poster.

## Make

- A headline a trader says out loud: their pain or the benefit, in plain words.
  A question works ("Why give autonomous agents raw wallet keys?"), so does a
  benefit ("Borrow without babysitting liquidation thresholds").
- One contrast the eye gets in a second: the problem card visibly breaks or
  strains, Vanna's card visibly holds. The reader should see the answer before
  reading a label.
- Two or three large cards. Each one shows a mechanism (a meter, a line
  crossing a threshold, a broken link, a shield holding), not just a title.
- One true figure at most, where it explains the mechanism, never as decoration.

## Do not make

- Protocol jargon in the headline or subtitle: monolithic, mutualized,
  socialized, blockspace, deterministic, primitives, segregated, unified
  margin. These read as an engineering spec, not a post.
- Wireframe diagrams: thin outlines, many small boxes, bars and brackets that
  explain nothing.
- Empty or filler cards: a card holding only a name ("Blend and Aquarius")
  and an arrow, or a title repeated inside its own card.
- A figure as the hero of the poster. A huge "1.25x" says nothing on its own.
- Org-chart or tree lines joining cards that are not a hierarchy.
- Several figures stacked as badges (gas, latency, trigger) on one poster.

## Never — basic errors and hallucinations

The founder handed over four posters on 2026-10-02 as "never make these"
(they lead the sent-back pile). What is wrong with them:

- The same thing printed twice: a card titled "~320ms indexing" that also
  holds "~320ms"; "0.00014 XLM gas" over a bar reading "0.00014"; a container
  and the card inside it both named "Isolated SmartAccount"; Blend and
  Aquarius drawn twice on one poster.
- Arrows that go nowhere: out of the frame, through a card, or into an empty
  box. A figure written on an arrow as its label.
- Icons and charts that explain nothing: radar rings, a random spike line
  inside a "trapped liquidity" card, a progress bar, red bars clamped on a card.
- A safe or vault for "isolated storage". (Already banned; it still appeared.)
- A grid of four cards that are each just a figure.
- Promises the facts do not support: "eliminate contagion and transaction
  failure", "Bank-grade assets cannot touch", "Transactions stay safe".
- Jargon in the footer too: "Deterministic risk isolation", "Built for
  deterministic credit". A footer left hanging on a dash.
- Absolute promises like "never touches your balance" or "never the pool":
  say what the mechanism does ("Only the account that breaks gets liquidated").
- A drawing that contradicts the headline: isolated accounts all wired into a
  "Core Lending Pool" in the middle.
