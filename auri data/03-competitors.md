# 03 — Competitive Landscape

*Research current as of August 2026. Every external figure carries a source at the bottom.
Re-verify before putting anything here into a deck or a pitch — this category is moving
fast.*

**Scope: Auri launches in the 🇺🇸 US and 🇨🇦 Canada, then goes global.** The two markets have
almost entirely different incumbents, so they are covered separately below. Read the market
you're pitching in.

---

## The headline

**Nobody competes with all of Auri. Everybody competes with one slice of it.**

Auri sits at the intersection of five separate markets. Each has strong incumbents, and
each incumbent is missing the other four legs:

```
            BUY REAL GOLD              🇺🇸 OneGold · Vaulted · APMEX · Costco
                   ╲                   🇨🇦 Wealthsimple · Silver Gold Bull
                    ╲                  🌍 Goldmoney · BullionVault · SafeGold
   SPEND IT ─────── AURI ─────── BORROW AGAINST IT
   Glint · Kinesis   ╱   ╲       🇺🇸 Coinbase · Nexo · Milo · Figure · Strike
   Gnosis Pay       ╱     ╲      🇨🇦 Ledn · APX   🇮🇳 Muthoot · Rupeek · IIFL
        SELF-CUSTODY       SEND / GIFT / REMIT
        Gnosis · Ether.fi   Wise · Remitly · Abound · Western Union
```

**The moat is the intersection, not any single leg.** That's also the strategic risk: an
incumbent who owns one leg and adds a second is a faster threat than a startup building all
five. See [Threat radar](#threat-radar) below.

## Tier 1a — 🇨🇦 Direct threats in Canada

### Wealthsimple Gold 🔴 **The most dangerous competitor in Canada**

Launched gold trading inside the Wealthsimple app: physically-backed gold, from **$1 CAD**,
24/7, fractional, real-time pricing, instant execution. **1% trading fee**, **$0 storage**,
custody via **Silver Gold Bull**, and — critically — users can **redeem holdings for Royal
Canadian Mint coins**. Can be held in a TFSA/RRSP.

| Where they beat Auri | Where Auri beats them |
|---|---|
| Millions of existing Canadian users, enormous trust | **Cannot borrow against your gold** — the entire Auri credit product doesn't exist there |
| Registered accounts (TFSA/RRSP tax shelter) — Auri has no answer to this | **Cannot spend it** — no card |
| Physical coin redemption is a powerful emotional hook | **Cannot send or gift it** to another person |
| Regulated brokerage; no liquidation risk to explain | **Custodial** — you're trusting their balance sheet, not holding keys |
| $1 minimum, dead-simple onboarding, no KYC friction beyond what users already did | No developer API / agent access |

**How to position against them:** *"Wealthsimple lets you own gold. Auri lets you use it."*
Do **not** fight on brand, trust, or ease of first purchase — you will lose. Fight on
**borrow, spend, send, gift, and custody.** Their product ends where Auri's begins.

**The nightmare scenario:** Wealthsimple adds a gold-backed line of credit. They have the
balance sheet and the licence to do it. Watch for it.

### Gold ETFs (CGL / CGL.C on the TSX)

Commission-free on Wealthsimple and Questrade, TFSA/RRSP-eligible. The default answer for
any financially literate Canadian who wants gold exposure. **No custody, no spending, no
borrowing, no gifting** — it's a paper claim in a brokerage account. Auri's counter is
*"you don't own gold, you own a fund that owns gold, and you can't do anything with it."*

### Silver Gold Bull / traditional Canadian bullion dealers

Sell you real bars and coins, ship them to your door. Highest emotional trust
("I can hold it"), zero programmability. Also now Wealthsimple's custody partner — which
tells you where the market is heading.

## Tier 1b — 🇺🇸 Direct threats in the United States

**The US has no Wealthsimple-equivalent.** There is no single dominant, trusted consumer
brand that sells fractional physical gold inside an app people already use. Instead the
market is fragmented between specialist gold platforms and giant brokerages selling paper
ETFs. **That fragmentation is an opportunity — the US gold-app market is winnable in a way
the Canadian one is not.**

### OneGold 🟠 **The strongest US incumbent**

A joint venture between **APMEX** (the largest US online bullion dealer) and **Sprott Inc.**
Fully digital: buy, sell, and hold vaulted gold, silver, and platinum from a phone, 24/7.
The most credible parentage in the category.

- **Pricing:** Switzerland Gold at **0% over spot**; other gold products **0.80–1.50%** over
  spot. **Storage 0.12%/yr** for gold. **Resell fee 0.30%.** Minimum **$100**.
- Vaults in the US and Canada (not Switzerland, unlike Glint and XAUT).
- **Custodial. No spending, no borrowing, no sending, no gifting, no API.**

**How to position against them:** OneGold wins on price and on bullion-industry credibility.
Do not fight there. Fight on the fact that **OneGold is a warehouse** — you can put gold in
and take gold out, and that's the entire product. Auri is an account.

### Vaulted 🟡

Same category, simpler: **one all-in fee of 1.8%** of the wholesale gold price (inclusive of
spread, premium, and commission), and a **$10 minimum** — the lowest entry point in the US
market, and notably the same starting point Auri markets. Custodial, buy-and-hold only.

*Note for pricing strategy: Vaulted at 1.8% and OneGold at 0.80–1.50% + 0.12% storage bracket
the US price expectation. It is a wider, more forgiving band than Canada's — Wealthsimple's
1% is a much harder anchor to beat than either of these.*

### US gold ETFs — GLD, IAU, and the brokerages

The default for any financially literate American: commission-free in every major brokerage
(Fidelity, Schwab, Robinhood, Vanguard), IRA/401(k)-eligible. **This is the real volume
competitor in the US, and Auri will not beat it on convenience or tax treatment.** The
counter is identical to the Canadian ETF argument: *you don't own gold, you own a fund that
owns gold, and you can't spend it, send it, gift it, or borrow against it.*

### APMEX, JM Bullion, Costco

Physical bullion delivered to your door. Costco selling gold bars to suburban households is
the single clearest signal that **mainstream US retail gold demand is real and growing** —
and every one of those buyers now owns an asset that does nothing.

**These are lead sources, not competitors.** Someone who just bought a Costco gold bar has
demonstrated exactly the intent Auri needs and has no way to act on it further.

## Tier 2 — Gold you can spend (Auri's closest product analogues, globally)

### Glint Pay 🟠

The clearest precedent for "spend your gold." UK fintech, launched a **Mastercard debit
card in the US**, usable in **210 countries**. You spend gold directly from a gold wallet;
Glint converts at point of sale in real time.

- **Fees:** 0.5% on buy/sell and on FX; no fee for a USD or gold purchase in the US;
  storage + insurance **0.02% monthly** (~0.24%/yr)
- **Custodial.** Your gold is on their platform.
- **No borrowing.** No credit line against your gold — at all.
- **No self-custody, no on-chain proof, no developer API.**

**Read:** Glint proves the "spend gold" behaviour is real and desirable. It has existed for
years without adding credit — which is either a signal that credit is hard, or that the
opportunity is still open. Auri's bet is the latter.

### Kinesis Money 🟠

The most feature-complete gold-money platform in existence, and the most direct
philosophical rival. Gold and silver tokens (KAU/KAG), a debit card, and — uniquely — it
**pays a yield on gold**, redistributing **57.5% of platform transaction fees** back to
users as six monthly yields, paid in physical gold. Transaction fee **0.22%**, among the
cheapest in the category.

| Where they beat Auri | Where Auri beats them |
|---|---|
| **Yield on gold** — Auri has no equivalent, and this is a real gap | No borrowing against holdings |
| Cheapest fees in the category | Not built on standard DeFi rails — closed proprietary system |
| Gold *and* silver | No developer API / agent surface |
| Live card, years of operating history | Auri's UX is Web2-clean; Kinesis is crypto-flavoured and complex |

**Take this one seriously.** Kinesis is what Auri looks like if it wins, minus credit and
minus self-custody. Their yield mechanic is the single best idea in the competitive set and
worth a serious product discussion — see [05 — Business model](05-business-model.md).

## Tier 3 — Vaulted gold platforms (the incumbents Auri makes look old)

| Platform | Fees | Spend? | Borrow? | Yield? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Goldmoney** | 0.5% buy + 0.5% sell (1.0% round trip) | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| **BullionVault** | Storage min ~$144/yr; redemption fees 2.5–10% | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| **OneGold** | ~$20/yr minimum storage | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| **OWNx / Vaulted / Kitco** | Varies | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |

These are *buy-and-park* businesses. They are Auri's easiest competitive story: **none of
them pay a yield, let you spend, or let you borrow.** Their weakness is opaque and punitive
redemption fees — worth attacking directly in comparison content.

## Tier 4 — Borrowing against assets (where the money is)

### Ledn 🔴 **The most important competitor to watch**

Toronto-based, the pioneer of crypto-backed lending in Canada. **Bitcoin only** — they
dropped Ethereum in late 2025 to focus exclusively on BTC. Max LTV **50%**. Not available
in **Quebec, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, or Saskatchewan**.

**Why this is urgent: Tether has partnered with Ledn to let XAUT holders borrow against
their gold.** That is *literally Auri's core product*, from the issuer of the exact token
Auri uses, distributed by an established Canadian lender.

**The mitigating detail that buys Auri time:** when the product launches, it will **not be
available to residents of Canada or the EU**. Rates, LTV, and minimums are undisclosed.
Expected later in 2026.

**Strategic implication:** Auri's window is real but explicitly time-boxed. Note the
asymmetry carefully — the Tether/Ledn product excludes Canada **but not the US**. So in the
US, Auri should expect a well-funded, fully-legitimate gold-lending competitor with the token
issuer behind it, possibly before Auri launches there. **In Canada the window is regulatory
and temporary; in the US there may be no window at all — only a race.**

### 🇺🇸 The US crypto-lending market — crowded, mature, and instructive

This is the biggest difference between the two markets. Canada has ~2 real crypto lenders.
The US has a whole industry, and it has already trained consumers to accept "borrow against
your assets instead of selling them."

| Lender | Collateral | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coinbase** 🔴 | BTC | **from ~4% APR** | Up to $1M USDC. **Built on Morpho, running on Base.** Reintroduced Jan 2025 |
| **Nexo** | BTC, ETH, 100+ assets | 1.9–18.9% APR | Relaunched in the US in 2026, **excluding New York**. $50–$2M |
| **Milo** | BTC, ETH | from 10.75% fixed | Crypto *mortgages*; $5k–$5M; **$100M+ originated**, incl. a $12M single loan |
| **Figure / FigureMarkets** | BTC, ETH | — | Institutional-grade lending to businesses and individuals |
| **Strike, Salt, Unchained** | BTC | ~5–14% | Bitcoin-native lenders |

Typical US market range: **~5% to over 14% APR, most between 8% and 13%.**

**Three things to take from this table:**

1. **Auri's 3.75% is competitive but not unique in the US.** Coinbase already lends from
   ~4%. The differentiator in the US cannot be *rate* — it must be **the collateral**
   (gold, not crypto) and **the customer** (savers, not traders).
2. **Coinbase built on Morpho.** Auri uses the same protocol. That is enormous third-party
   validation of the technical choice, and it should be said out loud to any investor who
   asks whether the rails are safe.
3. **None of them take gold.** Every single US lender in this table requires the customer to
   already hold crypto. The person with $30,000 in gold and no credit score is served by
   nobody.

### DeFi direct (Aave / Morpho) and APX Lending

- **Morpho / Aave directly** — a sophisticated user can do exactly what Auri does, manually,
  for less. Auri's answer is that ~99.9% of the target market never will: they'd need a
  wallet, gas, an understanding of LTV mechanics, and tolerance for a UI that assumes you
  read the docs.
- **APX Lending** — Canadian, crypto-backed loans, direct Ledn competitor.

### Indian gold-loan market (the size-of-prize comparison)

Rupeek, indiagold, IIFL, Muthoot, Manappuram — **8–15% APR**, branch-heavy, with digital
apps now disbursing in under 60 seconds against gold in a digital locker.

**Auri at 3.75% APR is 2–4× cheaper than the entire Indian gold-loan industry.** That is a
staggering number. It is the strongest data point for any future India play, and the
sharpest message for the Indian diaspora in **both** the US and Canada — a population that
knows exactly what a gold loan is and exactly what it should cost.

## Tier 5 — Self-custody spending (the pattern rivals)

**Gnosis Pay** — fully self-custodial Visa debit spending stablecoins from a Safe smart
account, with IBAN support, up to 5% cashback, across the EEA, UK, Switzerland, Argentina,
Brazil. **Ether.fi Cash** — self-custodial Visa where you spend, borrow, *or* earn against
ETH/BTC/stablecoins, with collateral still earning yield while you spend, in 76 countries.
Also **MetaMask Card**, **Tuyo**, **Coinbase Card**.

**These are the architectural rivals, not the asset rivals.** They have already proven the
exact pattern Auri is built on — self-custody until the moment of purchase, borrow against
collateral, spend it — but on *crypto* collateral. Ether.fi in particular does
spend + borrow + yield on one self-custodial card.

**The gap Auri fills:** none of them offer **gold**. Their audience is crypto-native; Auri's
audience explicitly is not. A saver who distrusts banks *and* distrusts crypto has nowhere
to go — that's Auri's customer.

**But note the direction of travel:** if Ether.fi or Gnosis Pay adds XAUT as supported
collateral, they get Auri's product overnight. Auri's defence is the Web2 UX, the local
fiat rails (ACH and Interac), and the fact that their brands are unusable for a non-crypto
audience.

**Geography note:** Gnosis Pay is EEA/UK/Switzerland/Argentina/Brazil — **not the US or
Canada.** Ether.fi Cash covers 76 countries. So in Auri's launch markets the self-custody
card competition is thinner than the global picture suggests, and Auri's card — when it
ships — would enter a relatively open North American field.

## Tier 6 — Adjacent: digital gold in India

Not a competitor in the launch markets today, but essential context for diaspora GTM in
both the US and Canada, and for the eventual India expansion.

- **80M+ Indians already invest in digital gold** — the behaviour is proven at massive scale
- **Jar** — rounds up daily spending into gold; partnered with **SafeGold**
- **SafeGold** — vaults with Brink's, powers many fintech apps
- **MMTC-PAMP** — record ₹3,926 crore in digital gold purchases in January 2026; digital
  gold is now 20% of its profit margins
- **PhonePe, Paytm, Google Pay, Tanishq** — distribution at national scale
- Regulatory uncertainty around digital gold in India is live and ongoing

**What this proves:** micro-savings into gold is a validated, enormous consumer behaviour.
**What Auri adds that none of them have:** self-custody, borrowing, spending, and
cross-border transfer.

## Tier 7 — Remittance (a feature Auri absorbs)

**Wise, Remitly, Western Union, Abound, WorldRemit** — plus the informal corridors. Auri's
remittance play is not to beat them on price but to make remittance a *side effect* of
holding gold: send gold, recipient gets local currency at mid-market. The strategic value is
that **gifting and remittance are viral acquisition loops** — every gift claim link is a
new-user funnel with a pre-loaded balance. See [04 — Customers & GTM](04-customers-and-gtm.md).

**Why this matters far more in the US than in Canada:**

- The **United States is the largest source of outbound remittances on earth** — ~$93B in
  formal flows in 2024, with inclusive estimates as high as $230B once informal channels
  are counted.
- **India received ~$137–138B** and **Mexico ~$68B** in 2024; US→Mexico alone was ~$62.5B.
- Global remittance flows were **$857B in 2025** and are projected to exceed **$900B in 2026**.

⚠️ **Regulatory flag — the 1% US remittance excise levy.** The US now applies a **1% excise
tax on certain remittance transfers**. Whether a gold transfer between two self-custodied
wallets is in scope is a genuine open question that needs counsel before any US remittance
marketing. It cuts both ways: if Auri's transfers fall outside the levy, that is a real
1% pricing advantage over Western Union and Remitly and a strong marketing hook; if they
fall inside it, Auri needs collection and reporting machinery it does not have. **Do not
assume the favourable answer.**

## Auri's honest weaknesses

Say these out loud internally, before an investor or a competitor says them for you.

1. **No brand trust.** Wealthsimple has millions of Canadians; APMEX and Sprott stand behind
   OneGold in the US. Auri has a landing page. Trust is the entire product in savings.
2. **No tax-sheltered accounts.** No TFSA/RRSP in Canada, no IRA/401(k) in the US. This is a
   structural disadvantage against every brokerage and every gold ETF in both markets.
3. **Liquidation risk is a hard sell.** "Your gold might get sold" is a genuinely scary
   sentence to a conservative saver. This is the #1 conversion objection.
4. **Not a bank, not CDIC- or FDIC-insured.** Legally correct and honestly disclosed — and
   still a trust obstacle for the exact demographic Auri targets.
5. **The rails aren't built — and now there are two sets of them.** No fiat on/off-ramp, no
   KYC, no card. Launching in both the US and Canada means **two ramp integrations (ACH/wire
   and Interac/EFT), two banking partners, and two regulatory regimes.** Until they work,
   the funnel has no top. **This is the single biggest execution risk, and going bi-national
   roughly doubles it.**
6. **Dependency stack.** Tether (XAUT), Morpho, Privy, ZeroDev, Enso, and future KYC/ramp/
   card partners. Any one going down or de-platforming Auri is an outage or worse.
7. **Thin XAUT liquidity.** The codebase already treats this as a hazard. Large orders will
   slip. It constrains how big a single user can get.
8. **No yield.** Kinesis pays gold yield; Coinbase, Nexo, and Ether.fi all pay yield on
   collateral. Auri's gold just sits there. Users will ask why.
9. **Concentration risk on Tether.** XAUT is the product's foundation, and Tether's partner
   Ledn is building the competing loan product — one that excludes Canada but **not the US**.
10. **US licensing is a potentially existential cost.** 49 states require money-transmitter
    licences; a national footprint is a **12–18 month, six-to-seven-figure** programme with
    application fees alone exceeding **$100,000**. Whether Auri's non-custodial architecture
    exempts it is *the* open question — see [05](05-business-model.md). Canada does not have
    an equivalent burden, which is a real argument for launching there first.
11. **Rate is not a US differentiator.** Coinbase already lends from ~4%. In the US the
    pitch has to be the asset and the customer, not the price.

gold-first consumer brand with the diaspora distribution, before a $50B company decides gold
collateral is worth a sprint.

## Recommended positioning (proposal, not decision)

**Lead with the credit line, not the gold.** Everyone sells gold. Almost nobody sells
*"cash against your gold, no credit check, 3.75%."* That is the sharpest, most
differentiated, most emotionally urgent message in the set — especially to immigrants and
self-employed people who fail credit checks in both countries.

**Sequence the message:**
1. **Hook:** "Need cash? Don't sell your gold. Don't get scored." → 3.75%, no credit check
2. **Trust:** "Auri holds $0. Your gold, your keys, checkable to the bar."
3. **Delight:** spend it, send it, gift it, stack it automatically
4. **Depth (developers):** "Gold your code can talk to."

**Then adjust the emphasis per market — the same product needs two different first
sentences:**

| | 🇨🇦 **Canada** | 🇺🇸 **United States** |
|---|---|---|
| **The gap you're filling** | Gold credit doesn't exist at all | Credit exists everywhere, but never against gold |
| **Lead message** | *"Wealthsimple lets you own gold. Auri lets you use it."* | *"Coinbase will lend against your Bitcoin. Nobody will lend against your gold — until now."* |
| **Primary enemy** | Wealthsimple's buy-and-hold ceiling | The gold ETF, and the warehouse model (OneGold, Vaulted) |
| **Killer stat** | 3.75% vs 20%+ credit cards, no credit check | Largest remittance market on earth + no gold-collateral lender exists |
| **Trust anchor** | Self-custody vs. a custodial brokerage | Swiss vaults + bar-level attestation vs. a paper ETF claim |
| **Don't fight on** | Brand, ease of first purchase, TFSA/RRSP | **Rate** (Coinbase is at ~4%), price per gram (OneGold is at 0% over spot), IRA eligibility |

**Do not lead with:** self-custody (too abstract for a mainstream saver), the technology
(nobody cares), or price (you lose that fight in both countries — Wealthsimple owns trust,
OneGold owns spot pricing, Coinbase owns rate).
